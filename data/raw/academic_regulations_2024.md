# MODEL UNIVERSITY ACADEMIC REGULATIONS
**INDIA • UGC-ALIGNED • AKTU-REFERENCED FRAMEWORK**  
**Document ID:** UAR-2026-V1.0  
**Proposed Effective Date:** Academic Year 2026–2027  
**Applicable Programmes:** B.Tech, B.E., B.Sc., BCA, BBA, M.Tech, M.Sc. and other eligible programmes  
**Regulatory Reference:** UGC regulations, NEP 2020, UGC Curriculum and Credit Framework, and applicable university ordinances.

---

## SECTION 1: PROGRAMME STRUCTURE AND CREDIT REQUIREMENTS

### 1.1 Academic Credit Definition
- One hour of lecture per week throughout a semester generally carries **1 credit**.
- One hour of tutorial per week throughout a semester generally carries **1 credit**.
- Two hours of practical/laboratory work per week throughout a semester generally carry **1 credit**.
- Credits shall be assigned according to the approved curriculum and applicable UGC framework.
- Students must complete the prescribed credits for their respective degree programmes.

### 1.2 Duration of Programmes
| Programme | Normal Duration |
|---|---|
| **B.Tech / B.E.** | 4 years |
| **B.Sc.** | 3–4 years |
| **BCA** | 3–4 years |
| **BBA** | 3–4 years |
| **M.Tech** | 2 years |
| **M.Sc.** | 2 years |

*Note: UG programmes may offer multiple entry and exit options where implemented under the applicable UGC framework.*

### 1.3 Semester Registration
- Students must register for courses at the beginning of each semester.
- Normal semester workload shall be determined by the approved programme structure.
- Add/drop requests shall be submitted within the university-prescribed registration period.
- Course withdrawal shall be permitted according to the academic calendar and applicable withdrawal rules.
- Students with outstanding backlogs may be subject to academic advising or registration restrictions.

## SECTION 2: ATTENDANCE POLICY AND SHORTAGE CONDONATION

### 2.1 Minimum Attendance Requirement
- Students are expected to maintain 100% attendance in lectures, tutorials, and practical sessions.
- A minimum of **75% attendance** is generally prescribed for eligibility to appear in end-semester examinations.
- Attendance shall be calculated using the total classes conducted and classes attended.
- For courses having separate theory and practical components, the university may prescribe separate attendance requirements.
- Attendance records shall be maintained by the concerned faculty and made available to students periodically.

### 2.2 Attendance Condonation
Universities may allow attendance shortage relaxation under their approved ordinances. A representative model is:

| Attendance | Eligibility Status |
|---|---|
| **75% and above** | Normally eligible |
| **65%–74.99%** | May be considered for condonation |
| **Below 65%** | Normally not eligible under this model |

**Condonation may be considered in exceptional circumstances, including:**
1. Serious medical illness or hospitalization.
2. Participation in officially authorized university sports.
3. Representation of the university in national/international events.
4. Other exceptional circumstances approved by the competent authority.

**Important Conditions:**
- Supporting documents must be submitted within the prescribed deadline.
- Condonation is not an automatic right; approval must be granted by the designated university authority.
- Applicable fees, if any, shall be determined by the university.
- *Note:* The 65% threshold is a proposed model, not a universal UGC rule. AKTU's own regulations and other universities' ordinances may prescribe different limits.

### 2.3 Attendance-Related Examination Restriction
- Students who fail to satisfy the applicable attendance requirement and do not receive condonation may be declared ineligible for the relevant examination.
- The university may assign an attendance-related failure status according to its examination ordinances.

## SECTION 3: EXAMINATION AND ASSESSMENT SYSTEM

### 3.1 Continuous Internal Assessment (CIA)
A university may adopt the following illustrative assessment structure for a 100-mark theory course:

| Assessment Component | Marks |
|---|---|
| **Mid-Term Examination 1** | 15 |
| **Mid-Term Examination 2** | 15 |
| **Assignments / Quizzes / Class Tests** | 10 |
| **End-Semester Examination (ESE)** | 60 |
| **Total** | **100** |

*Internal assessment components and their weightage shall be specified in the course syllabus.*

### 3.2 End-Semester Examination (ESE)
- End-semester examinations shall be conducted according to the approved academic calendar.
- Theory examinations may be conducted in written, online, or other approved formats.
- Practical examinations may include laboratory performance, viva-voce, projects, and practical records.
- Students must satisfy the prescribed attendance and examination eligibility requirements.
- Unfair means or examination malpractice shall be dealt with under university disciplinary regulations.

### 3.3 Passing Criteria
A representative university examination model prescribes:
- Minimum **40% marks in the End-Semester Examination** (i.e. at least 24/60 marks).
- Minimum **40% or 45% aggregate marks** in the overall course assessment (CIA + ESE combined), depending on the approved ordinance.
- Separate passing requirements for theory and practical components where applicable.
- Students failing to satisfy the prescribed criteria shall be required to reappear in the relevant examination.

## SECTION 4: GRADING SYSTEM AND CGPA CALCULATION

### 4.1 Ten-Point Grading Scale
The following representative 10-point grading structure is commonly used in Indian higher education institutions:

| Letter Grade | Grade Points | Performance |
|---|---|---|
| **O** | 10 | Outstanding |
| **A+** | 9 | Excellent |
| **A** | 8 | Very Good |
| **B+** | 7 | Good |
| **B** | 6 | Above Average |
| **C** | 5 | Average |
| **P** | 4 | Pass |
| **F** | 0 | Fail |
| **AB** | 0 | Absent |

*The exact marks-to-grade conversion ranges shall be specified by the university.*

### 4.2 Semester Grade Point Average (SGPA)
SGPA represents the student's academic performance in a particular semester:
$$SGPA = \frac{\sum (C_i \times G_i)}{\sum C_i}$$
Where:
- $C_i$ = Credits assigned to course $i$.
- $G_i$ = Grade points obtained in that course.

### 4.3 Cumulative Grade Point Average (CGPA)
CGPA represents the student's cumulative academic performance across completed semesters:
$$CGPA = \frac{\sum (C_i \times G_i)}{\sum C_i}$$
Here, the calculation includes all applicable courses and their credits across semesters, in accordance with the university's grading ordinance.

### 4.4 Grade Improvement
- Students may be permitted to improve grades through approved improvement examinations.
- Improvement opportunities shall be subject to university rules.
- Previous and improved grades shall be reflected in the transcript according to the applicable examination policy.

## SECTION 5: ACADEMIC STANDING AND PROBATION

### 5.1 Academic Probation
A student may be placed under academic probation if:
- The CGPA falls below the minimum prescribed academic standard.
- The student accumulates excessive backlogs.
- The student fails to meet the prescribed academic progression requirements.
- The university may assign an academic mentor to students placed under probation.

### 5.2 Academic Progression
- Students must satisfy the minimum credit and progression requirements to advance to subsequent semesters.
- Backlog courses must be cleared within the maximum programme duration.
- Students may be restricted from registering for advanced courses if prerequisite courses remain incomplete.
- Students who fail to meet academic progression criteria may be required to repeat courses or semesters.

### 5.3 Degree Classification
A representative degree classification system is:

| CGPA | Classification |
|---|---|
| **8.50 – 10.00** | **First Class with Distinction** |
| **6.50 – 8.49** | **First Class** |
| **5.00 – 6.49** | **Second Class** |
| **Below 5.00** | **Degree requirements not satisfied / Probation** |

*Note: These classifications are illustrative. Actual classifications, minimum passing CGPA, and first-attempt conditions must follow the university's approved ordinances.*

## SECTION 6: BACKLOGS, RE-APPEAR AND SUPPLEMENTARY EXAMINATIONS

### 6.1 Backlog Examination
- Students failing a course shall be permitted to reappear in the relevant examination as per the examination calendar.
- Backlog examinations may be conducted during subsequent regular or supplementary examination sessions.
- Students must complete all required academic components as prescribed by the university.

### 6.2 Supplementary Examination
- Eligible students may apply for supplementary examinations conducted according to the university's notified schedule.
- The applicable examination fee shall be paid before the prescribed deadline.
- Students must clear all backlogs within the maximum duration permitted for the programme.

### 6.3 Attendance and Backlog Restrictions
- Students who fail due to attendance shortage may be required to repeat the course, including its attendance and academic components.
- Merely registering for a backlog examination does not automatically remove an attendance-related restriction.
- The university's specific re-registration and attendance rules shall apply.

---

## SECTION 7: RE-EVALUATION, SCRUTINY AND GRIEVANCE REDRESSAL

### 7.1 Answer-Script Verification
Students may apply for scrutiny or verification of examination results within the prescribed period. Verification includes:
- Checking whether all answers have been evaluated.
- Verification of marks awarded and recorded.
- Checking for calculation or totaling errors.
- Verification of marks transferred to the result sheet.

### 7.2 Re-Evaluation
- Students may apply for re-evaluation wherever permitted by university regulations.
- Applications must be submitted within the notified deadline with the prescribed fee.
- Revised marks shall be updated according to the applicable examination ordinance.

### 7.3 Student Grievance Redressal
Students may submit academic complaints concerning:
- Attendance discrepancies.
- Internal assessment marks.
- Examination results or unfair evaluation.
- Examination eligibility, academic registration, and progression.
- Grievances shall be addressed through the designated institutional grievance redressal mechanism.

---

## SECTION 8: DEGREE COMPLETION AND AWARD

### 8.1 Degree Eligibility
A student shall be eligible for the award of a degree upon:
- Completion of all prescribed programme credits.
- Successful completion of all compulsory and elective courses.
- Clearing all outstanding backlogs.
- Satisfying applicable academic and administrative requirements.
- Completing the prescribed programme duration and other statutory requirements.

### 8.2 Maximum Programme Duration
The maximum duration for completing a programme shall be governed by the applicable UGC framework and university regulations.

### 8.3 Academic Transcript
The university shall issue an academic transcript containing:
- Course codes and course titles.
- Course credits.
- Letter grades and grade points.
- SGPA for each semester and overall CGPA.
- Total credits earned.
- Degree classification, where applicable.

---

## SECTION 9: GENERAL ACADEMIC AND DISCIPLINARY RULES
- Students must follow the university's code of conduct.
- Ragging, plagiarism, cheating, and academic misconduct shall be dealt with under applicable university and statutory regulations.
- Students must maintain academic integrity in assignments, projects, and examinations.
- The university reserves the right to amend academic regulations in accordance with applicable regulatory requirements.
- All academic decisions shall be communicated through official university notifications.
